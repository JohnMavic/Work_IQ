💡 Key Insights

🏆 Why v1a Wins

  • Structural clarity works. The Decision Framework gave Copilot unambiguous classification rules — raising Classification from 50 to 70 and Deduplication from 50 to 70.
  • Teams-specific rules closed the gap. Explicit Teams channel vs. chat rules eliminated the biggest source of missed items.
  • It preserved what already worked. Subject Fidelity (100) and Output Format (100) stayed perfect — no regressions.

❌ Why Every Challenger Failed

  • Content approaches (v1b, v1c): DO/DON'T examples and compaction hurt task discovery — Copilot's conservative bias means fewer examples = fewer found tasks.
  • Recall relaxation (v2a, v2b): Relaxing thresholds caused Copilot to return zero tasks — the model interpreted permissive language as "don't filter" rather than "find more".
  • Tone shifts (v3b): Switching from conservative to thorough framing dropped Subject Fidelity from 100 to 30 — the model lost precision when pushed to be inclusive.
  • Structural reordering (v4a, v4b, v4c): Moving sections around or adding surgical patches either had no effect or caused timeouts — the model was already at its structural optimum.
Core Insight: The Copilot model has a strong inherent bias toward caution in classification tasks. The only successful strategy was adding structural decision frameworks that gave explicit, unambiguous rules. Every attempt to relax, reword, or reframe the instructions either failed catastrophically (0 tasks found) or caused regressions. The v1a approach succeeded because it worked with the model's cautious nature rather than against it — providing clearer guardrails instead of looser ones.
